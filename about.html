About

I’m finishing a B.S. in Biology with a neuroscience concentration at Stony Brook University and continuing into the M.S. in Neuroscience. The question I keep coming back to is how the brain recovers after injury, and what we can build to help it.

That question has taken me through a protein biochemistry lab, a neurosurgery department, a public health team at Johns Hopkins, and now a neurogenesis lab, where I study how new neurons form in the adult hippocampus. Time in the clinic has shaped the questions I ask: watching craniotomies at Lenox Hill, rounding with hospitalists at Johns Hopkins, and consenting patients for studies in Stony Brook’s emergency department.

I also like building things. I mentored a FIRST Robotics team for three years, I’m on Stony Brook’s Robotics Team and a member of IEEE, and I now teach students in the biomedical engineering program to design and program circuits for biomedical devices.

Long term, I want a career that combines neuroscience research with patient care, especially for people living with neurodegenerative disease or traumatic brain injury.
